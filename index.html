What breast reduction can do for you

Most women come to us for physical relief. Many leave with something they did not expect: a sense of ease in their own body.

  • Relief from neck, shoulder and back ache

    Less weight pulling forward means less strain through your upper body every day.

  • Better posture without trying

    Stand tall with your shoulders back, rather than bracing against the weight.

  • Freedom to exercise

    Run, swim, dance or join a class without double sports bras or discomfort.

  • Clothes that fit

    Tops, dresses and swimwear that fit your bust and your waist in the same size.

  • Healthier skin

    No more rashes, soreness or sweat trapped in the fold beneath the breast.

  • A lifted, balanced shape

    Breasts in proportion with your frame, with nipples set at a natural height.

You are likely to be a good candidate if you

  • are in good general health
  • have been at a stable weight for a few months
  • do not smoke, or can stop for six weeks before and after surgery
  • want comfort and proportion, with realistic expectations

Is breast reduction right for you?

Breast reduction may be right for you if the size and weight of your breasts affect your body and your everyday life. Many women we see live with neck, shoulder or back pain that will not settle, deep grooves where their bra straps sit, and rashes or soreness under the breasts. Some avoid exercise because of the weight, struggle to find clothes and bras that fit properly, or feel self-conscious about their size. Others notice that one breast is noticeably larger than the other, or that their breasts became heavier and lower after pregnancy or weight change. If any of this sounds familiar, a consultation with Mr Khatib will confirm whether reduction mammoplasty is the right step for you.

How does breast reduction surgery work?

A breast reduction procedure, known medically as reduction mammoplasty, removes a measured amount of glandular tissue, fat and skin. The nipple and areola stay attached to a pedicle, a bridge of living tissue that carries their blood supply and nerves, and are moved up to a new position.

The remaining tissue is folded into a lifted, rounder cone and the skin is tightened around it. Treating both sides is called bilateral breast reduction, and Mr Khatib plans each side individually to correct any difference in size.

Around the areola, straight down and along the crease beneath the breast. Best for larger reductions, as it allows the most skin to be removed and the most lift.

Around the areola and straight down, with no scar in the crease. Suited to moderate reductions where less skin needs to be removed.

Reduction combined with liposuction to the sides of the chest and armpit. Refines fullness that a reduction alone does not reach, for a smoother outline.

Your questions, answered

Is breast reduction surgery safe?

Breast reduction is a well-established operation when performed by a specialist plastic surgeon in an accredited hospital. Like any surgery it carries risks, including bleeding, infection, wound healing problems, changes in nipple sensation and asymmetry. Mr Khatib reviews your health and medical history in detail and explains how each risk applies to you.

Is breast reduction surgery painful?

Most patients describe tightness and discomfort rather than severe pain. It is well controlled with prescribed medication and eases steadily over the first one to two weeks. Many women notice their old back and shoulder ache has gone almost straight away.

How long does a breast reduction take to heal?

Wounds are usually healed within two to three weeks. Swelling settles over six to twelve weeks, and your final shape and softer, paler scars develop over twelve to eighteen months.

Will I have scars?

Yes, every breast reduction leaves scars. They are placed around the areola, down the lower breast and, for larger reductions, in the crease underneath, so they are hidden by underwear and swimwear. Mr Khatib gives you a scar care plan and reviews healing at each follow-up.

Can I breastfeed after a breast reduction?

Many women can, because the nipple stays connected to the underlying tissue, but it cannot be guaranteed. If you plan to breastfeed in the future, tell Mr Khatib at consultation so he can discuss timing and technique with you.

Can I choose my new cup size?

You can tell us the size you would like, and Mr Khatib will advise what is achievable and proportionate for your frame. Cup sizes vary between brands, so he plans by volume and shape rather than a letter.

Will my results last?

Removed tissue does not grow back, so the reduction is permanent. Your breasts will still change with age, pregnancy and weight, so keeping a stable weight helps your result last.

When can I drive again?

Most patients drive again after about two weeks, once they can wear a seatbelt comfortably and turn the wheel quickly in an emergency. Check your insurer’s terms too.

Can I get a breast reduction on the NHS?

NHS funding for breast reduction is limited and criteria vary by area, so waiting lists can be long. Choosing private surgery means you choose your surgeon, your hospital and your date.
